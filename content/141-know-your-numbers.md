# Know Your Numbers: Blood Tests & Biomarkers

Most of this course is about inputs — what to eat, how to train. This chapter is
about **feedback**: the objective measurements that tell you whether it's working, and
that catch the silent problems before they become loud ones. Heart disease, low
vitamin D, iron deficiency, prediabetes, and low bone density all share a dangerous
property — **they have no symptoms until they're advanced.** The only way to know is
to measure.

The aim here is the sensible middle. On one side is the person who never checks
anything and finds out at 55. On the other is the "biohacker" testing forty markers
monthly and panicking at every wobble. **A handful of numbers, checked at sensible
intervals, interpreted in context** — that's the target.

> **Education, not diagnosis.** Reference ranges vary by lab, sex, and age, and a
> single out-of-range value rarely means much on its own. Abnormal results are a
> conversation with a doctor, not a verdict you reach alone.

## The markers that actually matter

Not every test is worth doing. These are the ones with a real payoff for a healthy,
active adult — grouped by what they tell you.

### Metabolic health — the most important cluster

| Marker | What it tells you | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HbA1c** | Average blood glucose over ~3 months | The key screen for **prediabetes and type 2 diabetes**. Rises silently for years. |
| **Fasting glucose** | Blood sugar in a fasted state | Cruder than HbA1c but standard. |
| **Fasting insulin / HOMA-IR** | Insulin resistance | Rises *before* glucose does — an early-warning marker, if you can get it. |
| **Triglycerides** | Circulating fat | High trigs + low HDL is the **insulin-resistance fingerprint**. Very lifestyle-responsive. |

This cluster is where the highest-value early warning lives. Metabolic dysfunction
builds for a decade before it's diagnosed, and it's the most reversible with the exact
tools this course teaches — training, losing visceral fat, fibre, and cutting refined
carbs and alcohol.

### Cardiovascular — the numbers that predict decades ahead

| Marker | What it tells you | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LDL cholesterol** | Atherogenic cholesterol | The causal driver of atherosclerosis (see the heart chapter). |
| **ApoB** | Count of atherogenic particles | The **best single lipid marker** if your lab offers it. |
| **HDL cholesterol** | "Protective" fraction | A marker, not a lever. |
| **Lp(a)** | A genetic, independent risk factor | Worth measuring **once in a lifetime** — it's largely fixed by genes, and a high value changes how aggressively you manage everything else. Rarely tested; ask for it. |
| **Blood pressure** | Not a blood test, but essential | The cheapest, highest-value measurement here. Silent, common, and treatable. Check it. |

### Nutrient status — the common Swedish shortfalls

| Marker | What it tells you | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **25(OH)D (vitamin D)** | Vitamin D status | Near-universal winter shortfall at Swedish latitude (see micronutrients & bone chapters). |
| **Ferritin (+ Hb, transferrin sat.)** | Iron stores | The one to check if you're **tired, a woman who menstruates, an endurance athlete, or plant-based**. Both deficiency *and* overload matter. |
| **Vitamin B12 (+ folate)** | B12 status | A genuine priority if you're **vegan** (see plant-based chapter). |

### Hormonal & general health — case-by-case

| Marker | What it tells you | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TSH (thyroid)** | Thyroid function | Worth checking with unexplained fatigue, weight change, or cold intolerance. |
| **Testosterone** | Only meaningful if **symptomatic** | Don't chase a number. Genuinely low + symptoms is a **medical** issue, not a supplement one (see "supplements that don't work"). |
| **hs-CRP** | Low-grade inflammation | Non-specific, but a persistently high value flags cardiometabolic risk. |
| **Liver & kidney panels, full blood count** | General health screen | Usually included in a standard panel. |

## Two traps worth naming

**The creatinine trap.** If you supplement **creatine**, it raises **serum
creatinine** — the marker used to *estimate* kidney function — without harming your
kidneys at all. A routine test can make a healthy creatine user look like they have
mild kidney impairment. **Tell whoever orders the test that you take creatine** (this
is important enough that the creatine chapter says it too).

**The single-value trap.** Biology is noisy. One slightly-off reading — after a hard
workout, a bad night's sleep, a big meal, or dehydration — is usually noise, not
signal. Trends over time matter more than any single number, and a genuinely
concerning value gets **repeated and confirmed** before anyone acts on it. Don't
rebuild your diet around one data point.

## How to get tested in Sweden

- **Via your vårdcentral (1177).** If you have symptoms or risk factors — fatigue,
  family history of early heart disease or diabetes, being in a risk group — your GP
  can order the relevant tests, and this is **subsidised** (within the normal patient
  fee). This is the right first stop for anything that might be a real problem.
- **Private testing (e.g. Werlabs and similar).** Sweden has a well-developed market
  for **self-ordered blood panels** — you book online, give a sample at a lab
  (often a Karolinska/Unilabs/SYNLAB collection point), and get results with
  reference ranges. Convenient and comprehensive, but **out of pocket**, ranging from
  a few hundred kronor for a basic panel to **1000–2500+ kr** for a broad one.
  Useful for curiosity and baselines; not a replacement for medical care when
  something's actually wrong.
- **Ask for the specific markers you want.** Standard panels often **omit ApoB and
  Lp(a)** even though they're among the most useful — request them by name.

## A sensible cadence

You don't need frequent testing. For a healthy, active adult:

1. **A baseline panel** at some point in adulthood — metabolic cluster, lipids
   (with ApoB), 25(OH)D, ferritin, plus **Lp(a) once**. Earlier and more thorough if
   you have a **family history** of heart disease or diabetes.
2. **Vitamin D and ferritin** as needed — vitamin D through the winter, ferritin if
   you're symptomatic or in a risk group.
3. **Metabolic and lipid markers every ~1–3 years** when normal; more often if a
   value is borderline or you're actively working to change it.
4. **Blood pressure regularly** — it's free and it's silent.
5. **DXA/FRAX for bone** only with risk factors (see the bone chapter).

More testing than this rarely improves anything and mostly manufactures anxiety.

## The takeaway

- The dangerous conditions — **prediabetes, high LDL, low vitamin D, iron deficiency,
  low bone density** — are **silent**. Measuring is the only way to catch them early.
- Aim for the **sensible middle**: a handful of markers, sensible intervals, trends
  over single values — not zero testing, and not obsessive monthly panels.
- The high-value clusters: **metabolic (HbA1c, triglycerides, fasting insulin)**,
  **cardiovascular (LDL/ApoB, blood pressure, Lp(a) once)**, and **nutrient status
  (vitamin D, ferritin, B12 if vegan)**.
- Watch two traps: **creatine raises the creatinine marker** (tell your doctor), and
  **one off reading is usually noise** — confirm before acting.
- In Sweden, use the **vårdcentral/1177** (subsidised, for real concerns) or
  **private panels like Werlabs** (out of pocket, for baselines and curiosity) — and
  **ask for ApoB and Lp(a) by name**, as standard panels often skip them.
